Session One · 6 January 2026

The Summons

Three letters

Bayushi Taigen was at Kyūden Doji, keeping company with his betrothed, Doji Shizue, and watching the Crane's finest duelists at close quarters, when a servant told him his mother had arrived. Bayushi Kachiko received him in a visiting room with a single piece of calligraphy on the wall and nothing else in it; she keeps her own surroundings plain wherever she does not need to be noticed. She had, she said, a missive and a mission. The task would prove either not worth speaking of or worth great honour, and she believed he could make it the second.

The scroll held two documents. The first was a summons from Doji Satsume, the Emerald Champion, first sent to Taigen's father, Bayushi Shoju; Kachiko had persuaded Shoju that Taigen was the one to send. It asked for help with a parallel investigation into a recent murder. The second was the family's own intelligence. Miya Satoshi, the Imperial Herald, was dead, and the Scorpion had reason, though no proof, to think his loyalties to the Empire had been mixed. Newer than that, and intercepted on Kachiko's road to Kyūden Doji: the Crane delegate Doji Toshiyasu had also been killed. Both men had been torn to pieces. Taigen, who had served on the Carpenter Wall, asked his mother to write to the Crab and ask whether anything similar had been seen along it. She agreed.

Isawa Endo had been sent into the forest shrines by his lord, Isawa Uona, to work on his invocations and, in Uona's intention, on his opinion of himself. Recalled to the castle, he told Uona the invocations were all but mastered. Uona handed him a letter from the capital. Doji Satsume had asked the Phoenix for someone who could examine spiritual disturbances in support of an investigation there. The language was careful, but Endo read it as a murder, with a creature of the Shadowlands the leading suspect. He bowed and told Uona that in a few months the Phoenix lands would hear his name.

At Last Step Castle, Kitsuki Hasumi was summoned to a private audience with Kitsuki Jusai, who almost never gives them. Jusai gave her a sealed letter for Seppun Hokuto in Otosan Uchi, a man he called an exemplar of the Kitsuki's own methods of investigation, and orders to join a parallel investigation under Doji Satsume. Reading the scroll, Hasumi took the measure of the arrangement. Satsume's yoriki, the Emerald magistrate Kitsuki Kagi, was already investigating in the capital. Satsume was opening a second inquiry with people drawn from across the Empire who were not magistrates. He appeared to trust Kagi completely. The subtext was that he was not sure Kagi's investigation had not been compromised.

The road north

Taigen travelled with Shizue, who keeps rooms in the capital, and one guard from each of their clans. Endo brought Isawa Renji, a boy of twelve or thirteen from his own school who has not yet come of age, to see something of the Empire beyond Phoenix lands. Hasumi travelled alone, attaching herself to merchant parties along the way. Each was given quarters in their clan's district of the city. Taigen had his guard sleep in the room assigned to him, and arranged matters so that anyone who wants to learn where he actually sleeps will have to work for it.

Seppun Hokuto

Hasumi found Hokuto through the records rather than by asking. His office is several rooms with the walls taken out, filled with tables; what hangs on the walls are diagrams and maps rather than art. He read Jusai's letter, slipped it into his kimono, and asked whether she had met Satsume yet. She had not. He told her not to tell Satsume she had met him, and not to tell him about the letter; once she had been briefed, she was to send word, and he would say when they could speak. Then he went back to his tables. Hasumi fixed his expression in her memory to think about later.

The Emerald Champion

The three met for the first time in Satsume's waiting room in the Crane district. Endo arranged to walk half a step ahead of the others. He found Taigen's black and Hasumi's gloves and scar hard to see past, and concluded that both had seen more real conflict than he had.

Satsume finished what he was writing before he looked up. They were to work together. They had a secured workroom in the Crane district and a second, more neutral one in the Forbidden City, down a side street just inside the gate among the service rooms. They could use his seal, within reason, to go anywhere and to obtain an audience with anyone short of the Emperor. They were not to interfere with Kitsuki Kagi's investigation or share anything with it. Their charge was two murders and one attempted murder of Imperial delegates and people close to the Emperor.

He stood and paced, and set out the facts in order; Hasumi recognised a memory technique. Miya Satoshi, Imperial Herald for more than twenty years, famously honest and possessed of a flawless memory, had the Emperor's close confidence. He was found in his private chambers in the Forbidden City, torn into three pieces. A few weeks later came Doji Toshiyasu, an Imperial delegate who normally kept quarters in the Crane district and was known for his tea ceremonies, where he brought hostile parties together and got them at least to hear each other out. He was killed in the Forbidden City and torn in half at the waist. A day and a half before the audience, Ikoma Ujiaki of the Lion, an Imperial ambassador who spends much of his time travelling in Lion lands on the Emperor's behalf, was attacked and survived. Satsume declined to repeat Ujiaki's testimony and told them to hear it from Ujiaki before he left the city. Their first task was to find the source of the killings. The more important one was to learn why these people were chosen, and whether others were marked.

Questioned, he added what he had. The bodies looked as though a great beast had mauled them, but no one had seen or heard a beast come or go, and all three attacks happened in private quarters that were closed, either locked or with a bodyguard outside. Satoshi had been buried; Satsume had custody of Toshiyasu's body. Toshiyasu's yojimbo had written that she heard a struggle and furniture moving, went in, and found her charge dead and the killer gone. Kagi's investigators had been through all three rooms and had been told not to remove evidence, though Satsume could not promise they hadn't. He was leaving the city in a day or two to follow a lead of his own, would be back within two weeks, and wanted a report waiting for him.

Theories

In the Forbidden City workroom, Hasumi wrote a reply to Hokuto. The letter came out badly, but she kept its surface harmless, sealed it with wax and sent it by a servant. Endo introduced himself and offered the Shadowlands. Taigen, keeping his voice low, suggested goblins or an oni. Hasumi said little. In her experience oni and the supernatural are blamed far more often than they are responsible, though they are real. Endo noticed the sash of a Hida Defender under Taigen's coat and was glad of it.

Taigen has fought goblins and trolls himself, but no oni. He has heard the stories, and one of them was not told around a fire: a Hida, the only survivor of a patrol, had described a troll that picked up the body of one of his comrades and used it as a weapon on the rest.

They agreed to work together before they split anything up. The order was Ujiaki first, while he was still in the city, then Toshiyasu's body before burial, then the three rooms. After that they would look at the guests at Toshiyasu's recent tea ceremonies and talk to his yojimbo.

The first refusal

Taigen had Shizue write to Ujiaki asking for an audience that evening or the next morning. The letter named him as the Scorpion Champion's heir. It said nothing about the investigation or Satsume's seal. The reply came the next morning: a courteous refusal on account of urgent travel, with an invitation to visit Ujiaki at the Castle of the Swift Sword, or else to wait for his return at some unspecified date.

Hasumi, in the meantime, had gone alone that night to the room where Toshiyasu died, showing Satsume's token at the gate. The two-storey building was empty; after the killing, people had found other places to be. The suite sits on the ground floor, set back from the street. The screen between its smaller room and the main room had been broken through from the inside of the smaller room. The blood on the floor and walls was all in the main room. Some things had been taken away, and she could not tell what. She committed the whole room to memory, so that if anything changes she will know.

When Taigen brought the refusal the next morning, Hasumi said the letter must have gone wrong. Ujiaki was too important to the investigation to let leave, and his reluctance to see them was all the more reason to see him. Taigen explained on the way what his letter had said. The session ended with the three of them walking into the Lion district, uninvited, arguing about how to begin.

Session Two · 20 January 2026

Interrogation and Investigation

The pattern

Bayushi Taigen was not with the others that day. Hasumi and Endo went over the timeline. Satoshi died about three months ago, Toshiyasu within the last two weeks, and Ujiaki was attacked within the last few days. The killer had gone from months to weeks to days.

Ikoma Ujiaki

They brought a gift. Endo had heard of a Crane sake called Cherry Blossom Snow, known well beyond Crane lands, and found a bottle and a vessel fine enough to present it in. A Crane gift for a Lion carried some risk, but he judged that something meant to be drunk would not give the offence of something meant to be displayed. Ujiaki's chamber was guarded by a very large man in Lion colours with touches of the Mirumoto, a Dragon family, in his dress. He stood in a guard's stance throughout.

Ujiaki is a big man with a mane of hair and a full beard. He greeted them loudly, recognised the vintage, had it poured for everyone, and spent several minutes on the sakes he likes and the ones he does not. It has a flavour people either take to or cannot abide.

Asked about the attack, he called it "the prank". It was an illusion, he said, meant to frighten him into running, and when he stood up to it, it dissolved. Satsume was making far more of it than it deserved. He described something out of the stories told to children about the Wall: taller than the room, nine feet or more, with a huge head, tusks and glowing eyes. With it came a chill that was not cold, only a sense of heat being drawn out of the body. He stumbled, recovered, struck back, and it was gone. It never touched him, he said. He had a modest bruise, and security had tripled.

Hasumi watched him while he talked. He was favouring one side. He was lying, fluently and without much effort to hide it, in a way that suggested he often embroiders; if anything, he seemed to invite them to say so. Reading his manner, she judged him shrewd, the sort of man more easily approached with directness than with subtlety. Endo noticed his necklace: a finger of jade worn down almost to a toothpick. Jade shrinks in the presence of the Shadowlands Taint. Then Ujiaki reached forward to top up his cup and offer theirs, and Hasumi saw that his left arm, held close to his side until then, was three or four inches too long.

When Endo admired the jade and mentioned what jade does, Ujiaki stroked it and called it his lucky piece, which had protected him more than once. Then he stopped mid-sentence and stared at nothing, and when he came back he had lost the thread. Hasumi, going along with his story, said that if it was an illusion her first suspect would be a Scorpion school of illusion. Ujiaki replied that even the Scorpion have standards. He also took up her suggestion of a shinobi, which he said he had not considered and which worried him more. He was leaving the next day, and he had packing to do.

What the jade means

Endo remembered what his instructors had said of people who came back from the Crab lands marked by the Taint: elongated fingers, spindly legs, bodies grown wrong. The fact that any jade remained suggested the danger was past rather than active. He also remembered a name: Asako Kikue, who had served with the Crab as a field healer and spiritual intervener and has first-hand experience of such cases. Whether she is still in the capital, he did not know. He kept it to himself for the moment.

What neither of them could explain was the lie. People permanently marked by the Taint are usually treated as veterans who faced horrors on everyone's behalf, respected even if others are uneasy around them. Hiding it did not fit. Endo offered to divine Ujiaki's future that night and see what the kami would show them.

Doji Toshiyasu's body

The body lay in a guarded room in the Crane district, laid out so it could be examined. Two people were already there. One was an older man in cream, perhaps in his early sixties, who introduced himself as Daidoji Masahiro and asked to watch. The other was Tsume Rin, who had been Toshiyasu's yojimbo. Endo told Rin he was sorry her master had been attacked and that she had not been able to protect him. Her face stayed still, but she flinched. Endo meant it as sympathy; she heard that she had failed.

Endo found no Taint and no spiritual disturbance of any kind; the body had been cleansed so thoroughly it felt bleached. Hasumi examined the body itself. It had been cut through diagonally across the torso, the spine torn and cracked, and the wounds packed to hold it together. The face was unmarked. From the torso to the upper legs were tears and slashes like claws. Separate from these were two shallow blade cuts, at the left collarbone and on the upper arm. Masahiro said Toshiyasu had been found with his wakizashi in his hand, and that he would be buried with it. Hasumi examined the blade and judged that the cuts were defensive. The wakizashi's edge had been facing Toshiyasu when it cut him, so he had been disarmed, or the sword had been turned in his grip and driven back against him, or some other blade was involved. The cuts would not have killed him. The tearing did.

Masahiro asked whether they believed it had been an oni. Endo said they were only considering possibilities, and asked whether the palace's protections should not have kept such a thing out. As they talked, Endo recalled a conversation overheard years before in Phoenix lands, where someone had argued that every clan has its deniable servants. The example given was a Daidoji Masahiro and his Harriers, who officially do not exist. If this was that Masahiro, he was the man behind the Crane's shinobi. Masahiro said he had come to the capital to take Toshiyasu home to his family and was more used to mundane explanations. The Otomo who had dealt with Satoshi's death had arranged the purification: a Phoenix representative and several Otomo shugenja. He understood that the first investigation had already got what it needed.

Endo then unpacked the wound where the body had been torn apart. Clustered around the base of the spine, under the dried blood, were pustules and small tumours like gangrene. They did not look natural. "This is looking less and less like an illusion," he said quietly. Rin confirmed that the wakizashi had been bloodied before it was cleaned and purified. Hasumi, looking at the blood around the wounds, concluded that every one of them had bled while Toshiyasu was alive. Both of them lost honour for handling the dead, and Masahiro offered them a place to purify themselves before they left.

The divination

In the Forbidden City workroom, with Renji watching, Endo set out a turtle shell, chicken feet, spices and water from a shrine, mixed them and threw them across the table. What came to him was blurred and slow. He saw a caravan entering Lion lands under Lion banners, and felt struggle and pain. Then he saw a low room lit by a fire, where someone Ujiaki trusted with spiritual ailments poured holy water into his mouth, and the pain and distress eased. The capital has the best healers in the Empire, at least in principle, and Ujiaki was going home to be treated. The obvious reading was that he did not feel safe in Otosan Uchi.

The teahouse

That evening Endo took a seat at the communal table of a teahouse, doing his best to be noticed, and Hasumi sat beside him. They fell into conversation with an Ide courtier of the Unicorn, who seemed downcast. She had taken tea with Toshiyasu in the week before his death. He had brought her together with someone she would much rather not have spoken to, Asako Kikue, to try to settle a dispute between them. It had not been settled, though not for any want of skill in the host, and they had parted on better terms than they arrived. She had also heard that Toshiyasu's last tea ceremony was with the brother of the Scorpion Clan Champion. She could not say what they discussed, and could not imagine it had been peaceful.

Session Three · 27 January 2026

Beneath the Forbidden City

Ide Mihoko

The Ide courtier was Ide Mihoko. The tea she described had been with the Phoenix inquisitor, and Toshiyasu's courtesy had made an unpleasant meeting bearable without warming it. Endo told Hasumi what he knew of Masahiro and the Harriers. Hasumi, who trained to counter shinobi and holds them in contempt, thought through what it would mean if shinobi were involved. One possibility was that Toshiyasu had been killed by the Crane's own shinobi, and that all of this was a cleaning-up. She had no evidence for it. As she saw it, either an oni was being made to look like shinobi, or shinobi were making their work look like an oni's.

Mihoko asked Hasumi about her past cases. Hasumi said she could not discuss active work, and talked instead about deaths that people blame on the kami or on oni, which almost always turn out to have ordinary causes: a slip on a hillside, a drowning, bad luck. Endo, who trusts the kami of fire and earth absolutely, disagreed with some heat. While he was worked up, he sensed something close by: a kami bound inside a medallion Mihoko wears on a cord, tucked into her clothes. It felt faint and distant but was in fact near and confined. It was a fire kami of some kind. It also came to him that the kami are limited beings, and that they report what they know much as people do.

Bayushi Aramoro

Taigen, meanwhile, had been kept by his betrothed and then found by his uncle, Bayushi Aramoro, who was pleasantly surprised to see him in the city and asked what had brought him. Taigen slipped him the Emerald Champion's writ out of sight of anyone watching. "Working for the Imperial Palace themselves," Aramoro said, and asked for his nephew's theories. Taigen said the only man he knew in the Empire who could tear a man in half with his bare hands was the one the Crab call the Great Bear, and he was on the Wall. Aramoro laughed behind his mask and said that fortunately they are much more civilised up here. He asked Taigen to keep him informed, and offered his help.

Taigen joined the others at the teahouse. Mihoko excused herself, and they compared what they had. Taigen's experience on the Wall filled in the jade. As long as jade stays pure, it protects almost completely against the Taint, against creatures of the Shadowlands and against the spiritual poisons that break minds and bodies. There are exceptions he has heard of but not seen: places deep in the Shadowlands that overwhelm it, certain cursed objects, some kinds of maho (blood magic). Ujiaki's jade looked to him like protection against a single, intense exposure that nearly exhausted it without failing. The distorted arm might be a different exposure from a different source, and to Taigen it seemed unlikely that the two were the same.

Miya Satoshi's rooms

They began with the oldest scene. Satoshi's rooms in the Forbidden City had been cleaned completely and were emptier than they should be. To Endo they were spiritually dead: the small kami that live in any place had been scoured out and had not yet come back. It was not the deadness of the Shadowlands, only of a thorough cleansing. Taigen read the traces that were left. The screen door had been replaced, and there were cracked floorboards and small breaks in the wooden pillars. From these he could follow a struggle from one room into the main area and on into Satoshi's private chamber, where he was found.

Hasumi noticed that one part of the floor, under a fine rug, sounded different. Beneath the rug were an iron ring and a latch, and a hatch opening onto a basement. As far as any of them knew, these quarters have no basements.

Below

The cellar was sparse: goods stacked against a wall, and sacks of rice. Endo slit one open. The rice had turned black and ashy, gave off a faint acrid smell none of them could place, and carried a spiritual residue Endo did not like. The silence down there was complete. One door led to a storeroom of metal containers. The other was barred from the far side. When Hasumi knocked, the echo came back from a long way off.

Taigen went up to fetch guards and sent letters: to the office of the Emerald Champion, to his uncle and to Shizue. Hasumi worked the bar loose with whatever she could find in the storeroom. Taigen went through first.

Beyond the door were tunnels: a main corridor that went on and on, branches off it, and ladders climbing into other spaces. Taigen scratched marks into the wall with a knife as they went. Near a corner, Endo found the remains of writing in blood, scrubbed at but not scrubbed away. There were two or three characters at most, enough for a name or a short command, and none of them could be read. This cleaning had been hurried, unlike the rooms above, and whoever did it had been moving in the same direction they were. The guards said they had never heard of the tunnels. The pots, jars and rice suggested ordinary service passages. Hasumi thought only people of very high rank could know they existed, and that anyone who used them for killing would need rank of their own.

Hasumi held a map of the maze in her memory. She and Taigen found another, more thoroughly scrubbed mark further on, enough to suggest a direction. Endo followed something else: the earth kami in the tunnels were doing restorative work, the way they do after something they dislike has passed, and their trail led another way. The others followed Endo.

The escape

For an hour and a half the passages ran away from the Imperial centre, the walls growing rougher and less used, until they came out into daylight. They were on a small stone platform on the cliff at the south of the city, outside the outer wall, above the river valley. Two samurai had been stationed there. One had had his head torn off; the other had been thrown down onto the rocks below. Birds had been at both, and Hasumi could say only that they had been dead at least a couple of weeks, perhaps much longer. Endo felt that a presence had been driven out from this place and had gone southeast.

The dead men wore Imperial guards' dress, the practical kind rather than the ceremonial. A ladder of about forty feet, fixed into the cliff, led up to a narrow path hidden in waist-high grass. It was used rarely but regularly, as if guards changed on a rotation. Under a bench Taigen found a box of sacks and peasants' clothes and hats, enough for guards to leave their post dressed as farmers. Whoever the dead men had really been, the conclusion was hard to avoid: this was an escape tunnel. The dead men's wakizashi had the standard hilt he knew from the Seppun guardsmen, with one difference: pieces of jade were set into the hilt, and they had turned black, as Ujiaki's jade had after a brief and intense exposure. They took the box and the one wakizashi they could reach. Endo wrapped the wakizashi to purify it before carrying it among their things.

The bridge

Back in the cellar they sent a guard to report the bodies and follow Taigen's marks. Then Bayushi Aramoro came down the ladder. "I heard you found something interesting here, nephew." He looked into the box and said: "Infiltrators." He had come about something else. Ikoma Ujiaki had left the city. On the outskirts, the bridge his party was crossing seemed to have collapsed, and Ujiaki and his men had died. "Quite the unfortunate accident." His sources had been part of the attempted rescue.

Endo had seen Ujiaki reach Lion lands and his healer. Now he understood something about fate. What he had divined was true in its way: it was what Ujiaki intended, and what the kami believed would happen. But another fate had overtaken it, and it had come as a surprise to Endo, to Ujiaki and to the kami themselves. He told the others not to speak to him and walked out. Hasumi, standing by the door, was thinking about who knew about the tunnel, and how high a secret it might be, and about the Scorpion in the room. She realised the door can only be barred from the tunnel side.
